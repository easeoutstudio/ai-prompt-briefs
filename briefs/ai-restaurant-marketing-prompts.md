## How to write a restaurant marketing prompt

Use the same structure every time (Directed brief (text, photo, video)):

| Part | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **ROLE** | Who the AI should act as for this job. |
| **CONTEXT** | Facts about your place, guests and goal. |
| **TASK** | The one thing you want made. |
| **CONSTRAINTS** | Limits on length, tone, format and what to leave out. |
| **OUTPUT** | The exact shape of the answer you want back. |
| **SUBJECT** | The dish, drink or place in the picture. |
| **SURFACE** | What the subject sits on and what surrounds it. |
| **LIGHT** | Direction, softness and color of the light. |
| **CAMERA** | Lens in mm, angle, aperture and distance. |
| **COMPOSITION** | Aspect ratio, framing and empty space. |
| **STYLE** | The overall look, kept realistic. |
| **AVOID** | Things the AI must not add or do. |
| **SHOT** | The type of video shot and its purpose. |
| **MOTION** | What moves, how fast and in which direction. |
| **DURATION** | Clip length in seconds and key timings in ms. |

### Dish descriptions
*Menu writing*

Use when: Your menu lines are bare names or long and vague.

```text
ROLE: You are a menu copywriter for a neighborhood cafe.
CONTEXT: Ember & Oat is a [CITY] cafe with a [CUISINE_STYLE] menu. Guests are [GUEST_TYPE]. Tone: [TONE].
TASK: Write a description for each dish below using only the ingredients I list.
DISHES AND INGREDIENTS: [DISH_LIST_WITH_INGREDIENTS]
CONSTRAINTS: 12 to 20 words per dish. Lead with the main ingredient. No invented sourcing claims, no words like best or famous. One cooking method or texture per dish.
OUTPUT: A table with columns Dish, Description, Word count. Then 2 dishes rewritten in a second tone for comparison.
```

Tip: Add one sentence about how a dish is actually made to get more specific wording.

### Weekly post plan
*Social posts*

Use when: You never know what to post on Monday.

```text
ROLE: You are a social media planner for a small restaurant.
CONTEXT: [VENUE_NAME], [CUISINE_STYLE], in [CITY]. Platforms: [PLATFORMS]. Posting days: [NUMBER_OF_POSTS] per week. This week we have: [NEWS_AND_SPECIALS]. Staff time available: [MINUTES_PER_DAY] minutes a day.
TASK: Plan one week of posts.
CONSTRAINTS: Mix of 3 content types. Each post needs a real photo or clip we can shoot in under 10 minutes. No giveaways or discounts unless listed above.
OUTPUT: Table with Day, Platform, Format, Photo or clip to shoot, Caption draft (up to 40 words), Best time slot to test.
```

Tip: Name your slowest day so the plan puts a stronger post there.

### Hero dish
*Food photo prompts*

Use when: You need a main menu or website image.

```text
SUBJECT: [DISH_NAME] as served at Ember & Oat, with [REAL_INGREDIENTS] visible and plated as in [REFERENCE_PHOTO_OR_DESCRIPTION].
SURFACE: [PLATE_TYPE] on a [TABLE_MATERIAL] table.
LIGHT: Soft window light from camera left, 5200K, gentle shadow on the right.
CAMERA: 50mm lens, f/2.8, 35 degree angle, 40 cm from the plate.
COMPOSITION: 4:5 ratio, dish fills 60% of the frame, empty space at top.
STYLE: Natural, realistic food photography, true colors.
AVOID: Ingredients not on the real dish, extra garnish, fake steam, text, logos, distorted utensils, extra fingers.
```

Tip: Match the plate and portion size to what the kitchen actually sends out.

### Steam rising
*Promo video shots*

Use when: You want a warm, close shot of a hot dish.

```text
SHOT: Close-up of steam rising from a hot dish, for a 3 second b-roll insert.
SUBJECT: A bowl of [HOT_DISH_NAME] with real [INGREDIENTS] on a wood table at Ember & Oat.
CAMERA: 85mm lens equivalent, locked off, 30 cm from the bowl, slight low angle.
MOTION: Steam drifts upward at slow speed, about 1 cm per second. No camera movement.
LIGHT: Dark background, soft backlight from the right, 4500K.
DURATION: 3 seconds, steam visible from 0 ms.
AVOID: Fire, smoke, extra ingredients, hands, text, logos, fast motion.
```

Tip: Ask for an 8 second version and trim the best 3 seconds.

### Reply to a 5-star review
*Reviews and messages*

Use when: A guest left a warm review and you want a personal reply.

```text
ROLE: You are the owner of a small cafe replying to a guest.
CONTEXT: Venue: Morning Crumb. The review text: [PASTE_REVIEW]. Reviewer first name: [FIRST_NAME]. Dishes they mention: [DISHES].
TASK: Write a thank you reply.
CONSTRAINTS: 30 to 50 words. Mention one specific thing from the review. Sound like a person, not a script. No promises of free items. Do not claim facts not in the review.
OUTPUT: Three versions with different openings, each signed [OWNER_NAME].
```

Tip: Rotate the three versions so replies do not look copied.

### Monthly newsletter
*Email, SMS and events*

Use when: You send one email a month to regulars.

```text
ROLE: You are an email writer for a neighborhood cafe.
CONTEXT: Venue: Ember & Oat. Month: [MONTH]. News: [NEWS_ITEMS]. New menu items: [NEW_ITEMS]. Events: [EVENTS_WITH_DATES]. Voice: [TONE].
TASK: Write the newsletter.
CONSTRAINTS: 180 to 250 words total. Subject line up to 45 characters, preview text up to 80. 3 short sections with headers. One main call to action: [CALL_TO_ACTION]. No fake urgency. Include a plain footer line with address and an unsubscribe reminder.
OUTPUT: Two subject line options, preview text, email body, and footer.
```

Tip: Keep the same three sections each month so readers know what to expect.

## How to use these briefs

1. Copy one brief and replace every [PLACEHOLDER] with your real details before you paste it.
2. Paste text briefs into Claude or ChatGPT, photo briefs into an image model, and video briefs into a video model.
3. Use only real prices, hours, ingredients and quotes. If you do not have one, leave the placeholder and ask the AI to flag it.
4. Check every allergen, price, date and claim yourself before anything goes public.
5. Make AI food images match the dish you actually serve. Attach a real photo as a reference when the tool allows it.
6. Change one line at a time, such as the tone or the word count, and run the brief again.
