## How to write an Etsy or Shopify listing prompt

Use the same structure every time (Directed brief: text and photo versions):

| Part | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **ROLE** | Who the AI should act as. |
| **CONTEXT** | Your real product facts and audience. |
| **TASK** | The one job to do. |
| **CONSTRAINTS** | Limits on length, facts and tone. |
| **OUTPUT** | The exact format you want back. |
| **SUBJECT / SURFACE / LIGHT / CAMERA / COMPOSITION / STYLE / AVOID** | The photo version: what is shown, where, how it is lit and shot, and what to leave out. |

### Etsy title with keywords
*Titles and tags*

Use when: You are writing or rewriting an Etsy listing title.

```text
ROLE: Etsy SEO copywriter.
CONTEXT: Before writing, confirm these facts. Product: [PRODUCT_TYPE]. Materials: [MATERIALS]. Size: [SIZE]. Color options: [COLORS]. Buyer: [BUYER]. Occasion: [OCCASION]. Search phrases I think buyers use: [MY_KEYWORDS].
TASK: Write 5 Etsy title options.
CONSTRAINTS: 120 to 140 characters each. Put the most important phrase in the first 40 characters. No repeated words, no all caps, no symbols. Use only the facts above. Do not invent materials, sizes, certifications or reviews. If a fact is missing, ask me first.
OUTPUT: A numbered list of 5 titles with the character count after each.
```

Tip: Paste your top 3 real search terms into [MY_KEYWORDS] so the first words match how buyers look.

### Handmade story
*Product descriptions*

Use when: You want a short maker story near the top of the listing.

```text
ROLE: Copywriter for handmade brands.
CONTEXT: Maker: [MAKER_NAME]. Product: [PRODUCT_TYPE]. How I make it, step by step: [PROCESS]. Why I started: [TRUE_STORY]. Materials and where they come from: [MATERIALS_SOURCE].
TASK: Write a maker story for the listing.
CONSTRAINTS: 120 to 150 words. First person. Plain words. Use only my process and story above. Do not invent awards, press, certifications, batch counts or reviews. Ask me first if any detail is missing.
OUTPUT: One paragraph, then one 12-word line to use as a pull quote.
```

Tip: The more specific your PROCESS notes, the less generic the story.

### Main image on white
*Product photo prompts*

Use when: You need a clean first image for a listing or marketplace.

```text
SUBJECT: My real product, [PRODUCT_DESCRIPTION], matching the uploaded reference photo exactly in shape, color, texture and proportions.
SURFACE: Seamless pure white background (RGB 255,255,255) with a soft contact shadow.
LIGHT: Large soft light from front left, fill from right, no color cast.
CAMERA: 85mm lens, f/8, eye-level slightly above, product sharp edge to edge.
COMPOSITION: Square 2000 x 2000 px, product fills 85 percent of the frame, centered.
STYLE: Clean catalog photo, true-to-life color.
AVOID: Changing the design, adding props, text, logos, extra parts, or smoothing real texture and handmade variation.
```

Tip: If the color drifts, add the exact color name or hex code from your product.

### Meta ad primary text, 3 versions
*Ads and social*

Use when: You are testing ad copy for one product.

```text
ROLE: Direct-response copywriter.
CONTEXT: Product: [PRODUCT_TYPE]. Real facts: [FACTS]. Audience: [AUDIENCE]. Price: [PRICE]. Offer, if real: [REAL_OFFER_OR_NONE]. Link goes to: [PAGE].
TASK: Write 3 versions of primary text, each with a different angle: problem, gift, craftsmanship.
CONSTRAINTS: 60 to 100 words each. First 125 characters must stand alone. No fake reviews, scarcity, countdowns or result promises. Do not invent materials, sizes, certifications or numbers. Plain language.
OUTPUT: 3 labeled versions, each with a 5-word headline and a 25-character description line.
```

Tip: Change only the angle between versions so test results mean something.

### Welcome series, 3 emails
*Email flows*

Use when: Someone just joined your list.

```text
ROLE: Email copywriter for a small handmade shop.
CONTEXT: Shop: [STORE_NAME]. What I make: [PRODUCTS]. My story: [TRUE_STORY]. Best sellers: [BEST_SELLERS]. Welcome offer, only if real: [REAL_OFFER_OR_NONE].
TASK: Write a 3-email welcome series sent on day 0, day 3 and day 7.
CONSTRAINTS: Each email 90 to 140 words, 1 call to action, subject under 45 characters, preview text under 90 characters. Email 1: welcome. Email 2: how it is made. Email 3: best sellers. No fake urgency, no invented reviews or numbers.
OUTPUT: 3 emails, each with SUBJECT, PREVIEW, BODY, CTA.
```

Tip: Add your real shipping times to email 3 to answer the first question buyers ask.

### Late order reply
*Customer service and store pages*

Use when: An order is behind schedule.

```text
ROLE: Customer care writer for a small shop.
CONTEXT: Customer name: [CUSTOMER_NAME]. Order: [ORDER_DETAILS]. Promised date: [PROMISED_DATE]. True reason for delay: [TRUE_REASON]. New realistic date: [NEW_DATE]. What I can offer: [REAL_OPTIONS].
TASK: Write a reply.
CONSTRAINTS: 90 to 130 words. Apologize once, be honest, give the new date, offer options. Do not invent causes, tracking details or compensation I did not list.
OUTPUT: Reply text plus a 1-line subject.
```

Tip: Add the tracking link if you have one.

## How to use these briefs

1. Open Claude or ChatGPT for text briefs, or an image or video model for photo and ad briefs.
2. Replace every [UPPER_SNAKE_CASE] placeholder with your real product facts before you send anything.
3. Text briefs ask for your facts first and will not invent materials, sizes, certifications or reviews. Check every claim against your product.
4. For photo briefs, upload a clear reference photo of your real item and compare the result to it before you publish.
5. Ask for one revision at a time, such as shorter, plainer or a different keyword focus.
6. Check platform rules and local law for policies, ad claims and reviews. The briefs are drafts, not legal advice.
